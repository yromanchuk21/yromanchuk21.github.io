I'm Yakov Vasilenko-Romanchuk, a mechanical engineer with a B.S. from California State University, Sacramento, and a Certificate in Scientific Computing from its Physics Department. My background spans industrial automation, optical inspection systems, embedded hardware, and IT infrastructure.

As a field service engineer specializing in industrial sorting machinery, I commission and troubleshoot production equipment, calibrate and align laser and hyperspectral imaging systems, develop sorting recipes, and work with hardware and software to solve machine-level problems. I also bring two years of experience in IT network and server administration, including Active Directory, Linux servers, and Ubiquiti enterprise networking.

Outside work, I explore embedded systems, hardware reverse engineering, and cybersecurity through hands-on projects. I designed and threat-modeled an [environmental monitoring system](/projects/environmental-monitoring-system/) built around ESP32-C3 sensor nodes, a Raspberry Pi server, MQTT, and network segmentation. During the security review, I identified a device-impersonation risk caused by shared credentials and addressed it by implementing per-device certificates and topic-level access controls.

My current projects include developing a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W-based hardware reconnaissance tool for investigating UART, JTAG, wireless interfaces, and other embedded communication protocols. I'm also expanding my knowledge of hardware security, industrial networks, and security monitoring while working through the Hack The Box Junior Cybersecurity Analyst pathway.

My long-term goal is to work in operational technology (OT) and industrial control systems (ICS) security, applying my experience with physical machinery, embedded electronics, and network infrastructure to securing the systems that operate industrial environments.
